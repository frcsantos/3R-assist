# About Assist3R (English translation)

Oct 9, 2026 · @Leo

## About Assist3R

**Assist3R** is an online tool that looks for methods capable of replacing animal use in experiments (its main focus), reducing the number of animals, or refining procedures. These are the so-called **3Rs (*Replacement, Reduction, Refinement*)**.

Starting from a free-text protocol, in Portuguese or English, the tool uses artificial intelligence to identify the experiment's parameters and returns two types of results: already recognized alternative methods approved by [CONCEA](https://antigo.mctic.gov.br/mctic/opencms/institucional/concea/old-index.html) (Brazil's National Council for the Control of Animal Experimentation), and scientific studies that use animal-free approaches for similar objectives. Each result links to the original source.

The tool was designed for researchers, faculty, and members of Animal Use Ethics Committees (CEUAs). Its goal is to help answer, before any use of animals, whether that use is truly necessary. The results serve as input, but the decision remains with the user.

## Two sources of evidence

- **Recognized methods (Basket 1):** a curated database of methods with scientific or regulatory recognition, recognized by CONCEA. Records are built from normative resolutions and official protocols and reviewed by experts.
- **Emerging evidence (Basket 2):** a search of the scientific literature (PubMed) for models and approaches relevant to the experiment's hypothesis or outcome, even if they have not yet undergone formal validation and regulatory approval.

This organization adapts the 3 Baskets model ([Kleinschmidt-Dörr et al., 2026](https://www.altex.org/index.php/altex/article/view/2989/)) to the planning and evaluation of projects.

## Principles

- **Traceability:** every recommendation points to the sources that support it.
- **Differentiation of evidence:** recognized methods and emerging approaches are not presented as equivalent.
- **Support, not automation:** judgment about scientific adequacy, the necessity of animal use, and ethical implications remains human.
- **Explicit uncertainty:** not finding an alternative does not prove that none exists; scientific similarity does not, by itself, prove that an approach is an adequate substitute.

## How to use

1. **Analyze:** Paste the protocol as free text or upload a file (PDF, HTML, or TXT).
2. **Parameters:** Review the extracted fields, the confidence badges, and the passages highlighted as evidence. Edit if necessary and confirm.
3. **Results:** View the recognized methods ranked by 3Rs and % match, with details, jurisdictions, and sources. Filter by 3Rs or jurisdiction.
4. **Literature:** For each experiment, open the PubMed-based analysis: outcome hypothesis, assessment of the necessity of animal use, and ranked supporting articles, with citations.
5. **Explore:** Browse the database of Methods, Regulations, and Documents. Use the **!** on any card to send feedback about that item.
6. **Glossary:** Look up domain terms.

## Origin

Assist3R emerged in 2026 at an AI hackathon for animal causes organized by [Code for Compassion](https://www.codeforcompassion.com/) and [Kickstarting for Good](https://kickstartingforgood.org/), based on a problem identified by the [Fórum Nacional de Proteção e Defesa Animal](https://forumanimal.org/) (Brazilian National Forum for Animal Protection and Defense): making it easier to identify methods capable of replacing animals in science and teaching. Since then, the project has moved beyond being merely a search engine for alternative methods and now also supports evaluating the necessity of animal use itself.
